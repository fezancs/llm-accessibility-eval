\subsection{RQ3: Judging alt text, and alt-text quality (C4)}
\textbf{Agreement with human ratings.} Against the mean BLV rating (Table~\ref{tab:cmbpe}),
pointwise scores correlated at $r={}$\res{r-blv-local-qwen25vl-7b} for \QV{} (CI
\res{r-blv-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}), \res{r-blv-local-gemma3-4b} for \GM{} (CI
\res{r-blv-local-gemma3-4b-ci}) and \res{r-blv-local-ministral3-8b} for \MI{} (CI
\res{r-blv-local-ministral3-8b-ci}, on \res{r-blv-local-ministral3-8b-n} items only). The CMBPE
panel (mean of per-judge z-scores) reached \res{r-blv-cmbpe} (CI \res{r-blv-cmbpe-ci}), the naive
panel (mean raw score) \res{r-blv-naive} (CI \res{r-blv-naive-ci}), and description length alone
\res{r-blv-length} (CI \res{r-blv-length-ci}). Against crowd ratings, CMBPE reached
\res{r-crowd-cmbpe} (CI \res{r-crowd-cmbpe-ci}), the naive panel \res{r-crowd-naive} and length
\res{r-crowd-length} (CI \res{r-crowd-length-ci}). All correlations were lower for crowd
ratings (CMBPE drop: \res{r-crowd-minus-blv-cmbpe}, CI \res{r-crowd-minus-blv-cmbpe-ci}).
The jury tracked human ratings no better than description length. On crowd ratings length was
ahead of the CMBPE panel (difference \res{r-crowd-length-minus-cmbpe}, CI
\res{r-crowd-length-minus-cmbpe-ci}, paired resamples); on BLV ratings no difference was
detectable (\res{r-blv-length-minus-cmbpe}, CI \res{r-blv-length-minus-cmbpe-ci}). On the
\res{r-blv-cmbpe-common-n} BLV items that all three judges scored, \MI{} remained the best single
judge (\res{r-blv-local-ministral3-8b-common}, CI \res{r-blv-local-ministral3-8b-common-ci}).

\textbf{Known-answer pairs (E3b).} On the \res{e3b-pairs} known-answer pairs, CMBPE chose the
original with accuracy \res{acc-known-cmbpe} (CI \res{acc-known-cmbpe-ci}), the single judges
\res{acc-known-local-gemma3-4b} (\GM), \res{acc-known-local-qwen25vl-7b} (\QV) and
\res{acc-known-local-ministral3-8b} (\MI, CI \res{acc-known-local-ministral3-8b-ci}), and the
naive majority \res{acc-known-naive} (CI \res{acc-known-naive-ci}). CMBPE beat the median single
judge by \res{h4b-stat} (CI \res{h4b-stat-ci}, Holm-adjusted $p={}$\res{h4b-padj}), so H4b is
\res{h4b-result}. The gain is small but real. It comes from three small models, and CMBPE uses
both orders of each pair while the baselines use one, so it does not isolate the bias
adjustment. CMBPE also beat the naive majority panel (difference
\res{acc-known-cmbpe-minus-naive}, CI \res{acc-known-cmbpe-minus-naive-ci}), most clearly on
descriptions with an appended sentence from another image (\res{acc-known-append-cmbpe-minus-naive},
CI \res{acc-known-append-cmbpe-minus-naive-ci}).
Truncation to three words was the hardest degradation for every method (CMBPE
\res{acc-known-truncate-cmbpe}, CI \res{acc-known-truncate-cmbpe-ci}; single judges
\res{acc-known-truncate-local-qwen25vl-7b} to \res{acc-known-truncate-local-ministral3-8b}; naive
panel \res{acc-known-truncate-naive}), with overlapping intervals.

\textbf{BLV-preference pairs (E3a).} Only \res{e3a-pairs} pairs have a side preferred by BLV
readers, so the intervals are wide. Agreement with that side was
\res{acc-blvpairs-local-qwen25vl-7b} for \QV{} (CI \res{acc-blvpairs-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}),
\res{acc-blvpairs-cmbpe} for CMBPE (CI \res{acc-blvpairs-cmbpe-ci}), \res{acc-blvpairs-naive} for
the naive panel, \res{acc-blvpairs-local-ministral3-8b} for \MI{} (CI
\res{acc-blvpairs-local-ministral3-8b-ci}) and \res{acc-blvpairs-local-gemma3-4b} for \GM{} (CI
\res{acc-blvpairs-local-gemma3-4b-ci}). All intervals overlap, and those of \MI{} and \GM{}
include chance level, so these pairs do not rank the methods.

\textbf{Generator comparison (E3c).} After recusal, \res{e3c-aggregated} of \res{e3c-valid} valid
verdicts on \res{e3c-pairs} pairs (\res{e3c-images} images) entered the fit. $S_4$ was
\res{s4-local-ministral3-8b} for \MI{} (CI \res{s4-local-ministral3-8b-ci}),
\res{s4-local-gemma3-4b} for \GM{} (CI \res{s4-local-gemma3-4b-ci}) and \res{s4-local-qwen25vl-7b}
for \QV{} (CI \res{s4-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}). Both \MI{} and \GM{} wrote better alt text than
\QV{} (differences \res{s4-diff-local-ministral3-8b-local-qwen25vl-7b}, CI
\res{s4-diff-local-ministral3-8b-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}, and
\res{s4-diff-local-gemma3-4b-local-qwen25vl-7b}, CI
\res{s4-diff-local-gemma3-4b-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}); \GM{} and \MI{} did not differ
(\res{s4-diff-local-gemma3-4b-local-ministral3-8b}, CI
\res{s4-diff-local-gemma3-4b-local-ministral3-8b-ci}). The ranking follows text length: the mean
alt text had \res{e3c-meanlen-local-ministral3-8b} characters for \MI,
\res{e3c-meanlen-local-gemma3-4b} for \GM{} and \res{e3c-meanlen-local-qwen25vl-7b} for \QV. The
model removes each judge's length preference ($\gamma_j$), but because the length gap between two
generators is systematic and each pair has a single non-recused judge, length bias and quality
are only weakly separable here, and longer descriptions may also be genuinely more informative.
H2 is \res{h2-result}; it needs a closed generator.

\textbf{Judge biases (E3c).} \GM{} and \QV{} favoured the second-shown text
($\delta={}$\res{delta-local-gemma3-4b}, CI \res{delta-local-gemma3-4b-ci};
\res{delta-local-qwen25vl-7b}, CI \res{delta-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}); \MI's interval includes 0.
Only \GM{} had a length bias, towards longer text ($\gamma={}$\res{gamma-local-gemma3-4b}, CI
\res{gamma-local-gemma3-4b-ci}); the other two intervals include 0. Position consistency was
\res{pc-local-qwen25vl-7b} (\QV), \res{pc-local-gemma3-4b} (\GM) and \res{pc-local-ministral3-8b}
(\MI). \QV{} and \GM{} preferred their own family's alt text
($\eta={}$\res{eta-local-qwen25vl-7b}, CI \res{eta-local-qwen25vl-7b-ci}; \res{eta-local-gemma3-4b},
CI \res{eta-local-gemma3-4b-ci}); \MI's interval includes 0. H4a is \res{h4a-result}
(Holm-adjusted $p={}$\res{h4a-padj}), so recusal matters: without it, these two judges would raise
their own family's score. Agreement between judges was low: Krippendorff's $\alpha$ was
\res{kalpha-e3b} (CI \res{kalpha-e3b-ci}) on known-answer pairs and \res{kalpha-e3c} (CI
\res{kalpha-e3c-ci}) on generated alt text.

\textbf{Validity.} Anonymisation did not hide the generator: a character n-gram classifier
identified its family with accuracy \res{leak-acc} (CI
\res{leak-acc-ci}, \res{leak-n} texts), against a majority baseline of \res{leak-majority}. Here
recusal, not anonymisation, keeps self-preference out of $S_4$. \MI{} gave no usable pointwise
score for \res{pw-missing-blv-local-ministral3-8b} of the 68 BLV and
\res{pw-missing-crowd-local-ministral3-8b} of the 204 crowd items, mostly because it returned an
object of per-criterion ratings instead of the single number asked for, even after the retry.
Its correlations therefore cover fewer items than the others'. With three families, each E3c
pair keeps one non-recused judge, so $S_4$ mixes alt-text quality with that judge's preferences;
\MI{} versus \QV{} rests on \GM{}, the judge with the largest position and length
biases. \GM{} wrote no parseable alt text for \res{e3gen-parse-error-local-gemma3-4b} images,
even after the retry, so its $S_4$ rests on \res{e3gen-ok-local-gemma3-4b} images.
